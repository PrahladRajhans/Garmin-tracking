# Build a local-first Garmin MCP, not another wrapper

The best free connector for a Forerunner 955 is a **three-layer system**. A sync daemon pulls from **Garmin's new official Personal API** first and from the unofficial python-garminconnect library second, as an optional add-on that is allowed to break. It writes raw JSON and parsed FIT files into a local SQLite/DuckDB store. An analytics layer computes the open, published metrics that paid products charge for: PMC (CTL/ATL/TSB), HRV baselines, a transparent readiness score, pace/power curves, critical speed, VDOT/Riegel predictions, decoupling, intensity distribution and monotony. A **thin MCP server with roughly 12-20 intent-based tools** reads only from that store. MCP is the right *interface* to the AI assistant, but it is the wrong place to put data access. Most existing Garmin MCPs call Garmin live through unofficial logins, and Garmin broke all of those in March 2026. The paid comparators show where the value is. Fitness AI Connector is mostly raw access plus workout push. AthleteData's moat is its derived analytics, memory and dedupe, spread over roughly 250 tools, far too many to fit comfortably in an LLM's context. A personal build can match the useful core of both for free. The one exception is pushing structured workouts to the watch, which officially needs a business-tier Garmin API. Vendor sites were blocked during research, so several claims below come from search snippets only. Those are flagged as **[unverified]** or **[snippet]**.

## What the paid connectors and analytics apps actually sell

**Fitness AI Connector** is a hosted, Garmin-only remote MCP with about 18 tools. They cover activities, activity details, FIT download, health summary, body composition, cycle data, trends, a refresh tool, structured-workout create/list/delete, and subscription and account management. The tool names were observed directly in this session. It signs in through Garmin's own OAuth. It also enforces Garmin Connect Developer Program attribution rules: a "Data from Garmin" header, the device name on each activity, and a no-endorsement footer. Together these strongly suggest it is an official business-API partner ([fmp.it.com snippet](https://fmp.it.com/en/fitness-ai/); [mcp.so](https://mcp.so/server/fitness-ai-connector/fmp-projects)). It has **no derived analytics tools**: no CTL/ATL, no curves, no predictions. Analysis is left to the LLM. A "Developer Option" reportedly costs **+$10/month**. The base price could not be verified **[unverified]**.

**AthleteData** is the broadest product found. It aggregates 20+ sources: Garmin, Apple Health, Oura, Whoop, Withings, Wahoo, intervals.icu, TrainingPeaks, Hevy, Concept2, Terra and more. Cross-source tools merge the sources and "count duplicate sessions once". On top sits a derived layer: get_pmc_status, get_readiness_today, get_load_flag, get_intensity_distribution, get_efficiency_trend, get_pace_curve/power_curve, get_race_prediction, get_anomalies, analyze_dose_response and reality_check_goal. It also keeps persistent athlete memory (get_athlete_context, coaching notes, thresholds) and writes back to the watch, TrainingPeaks and intervals.icu (tool listing observed in session; [athletedata.health/mcp snippet](https://www.athletedata.health/mcp)). Its algorithms, its Garmin access method and its pricing are all undisclosed. Only a 7-day trial is confirmed.

The consumer apps publish concepts more often than formulas. **Whoop** Strain is a logarithmic 0-21 scale of cardiovascular and muscular load. Recovery is a 0-100% score driven by HRV and RHR, with bands at 67% and 34% ([Whoop Strain](https://whoop.com/thelocker/how-does-whoop-strain-work-101); [Whoop Recovery](https://www.whoop.com/us/fr/thelocker/how-does-whoop-recovery-work-101/)). Healthspan/Whoop Age uses 9 undisclosed metrics after 21 recoveries ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.in/pages/how-it-works)). **Athlytic** documents the most among Apple Health apps. It compares sleep-window HRV (weighted more heavily) and RHR against a rolling 60-day baseline ([Athlytic](https://athlyticapp.helpscoutdocs.com/article/20-understanding-recovery)). **Gentler Streak** uses a TSB model driven by workout HR ([TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/features/deep-dive-gentler-streak/?amp=1)). **Oura** Readiness combines HRV balance (the last 14 days against a 3-month average), RHR, temperature, sleep and activity balance ([Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057791533)).

The open platforms are where the replicable methods live. intervals.icu offers a PMC and an eFTP estimated from maximal efforts ([intervals.icu](https://www.intervals.icu/features/fitness-chart/)). Runalyze offers Effective VO2max from HR versus pace, TRIMP-driven ATL/CTL over 7 and 42 days, and a "Marathon Shape" volume correction for race predictions ([Runalyze](https://blog.runalyze.com/tutorial/runalyze-understanding-the-calculations/); [Marathon shape](https://runalyze.com/glossary/marathon-shape)). TrainingPeaks offers TSS/rTSS/hrTSS ([TrainingPeaks](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/204071944)). Stryd offers RSS anchored to Critical Power ([Stryd](https://help.stryd.com/en/articles/6879537-running-stress-score-rss)).

**Garmin Connect** itself, on the FR955, computes Firstbeat composites: Training Readiness, Training Status, Acute Load, Recovery Time, Endurance and Hill Score, VO2max and race predictions. It publishes no weightings ([Garmin FR955 manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals-apac/webhelp/forerunner955/EN-SG/GUID-30B699EB-48E7-411C-ADC5-90A94CA8E4AE-1590.html)). Strava's Relative Effort and Fitness & Freshness formulas are also unpublished ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2019/03/28/strava-relative-effort/)).

User forums point the same way. Athletes trust simple trended signals (sleep duration, RHR, HRV against baseline) more than black-box readiness scores, and some turn Garmin's Training Readiness off entirely ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/incorporating-garmin-training-readiness-data-into-tr/115381); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/au/wellness/fitness/an-exercise-recovery-expert-says-this-is-the-metric-you-should-look-at-and-its-not-your-readiness-score)). The design lesson is to **show the inputs behind every score**.

## Garmin's October 2026 Personal API reshapes the access question

The routes into Garmin data differ sharply in legality, robustness and coverage.

| Route | Cost / legality | What it gives an FR955 owner | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Garmin Personal API key** (new, ~Oct 2026) | Free, official, personal non-commercial only | Activity JSON + original .FIT, Health JSON, Women's Health; pull-only | History caps (steps/stress 28 d, HRV 365 d), key expiry ≤1 yr; advanced metrics probably absent |
| **python-garminconnect** (unofficial) | Free, ToS grey zone | 348 methods incl. Training Readiness/Status, Endurance/Hill Score, race predictions, lactate threshold, workout upload | Breaks when Garmin changes SSO (March 2026 precedent); ban risk from frequent logins |
| **FIT files** | Free via either route above | Deepest per-activity data: 1 s records, laps, running dynamics, power, RR intervals if HRV logging is on | Parsing only; needs a source |
| **GDPR bulk export** | Free, manual | One-off full history backfill | Not continuous [unverified contents] |
| **intervals.icu** | Free, official Garmin partner, personal API key | Activities + wellness incl. HRV, plus its own PMC/eFTP | Adds a dependency; Garmin-only metrics partly lost |
| **Apple Health / Health Connect** | Free | Subset only (steps, HR, sleep, workouts) | Loses Body Battery, readiness and other Garmin-native metrics [unverified] |
| **Strava** | Free API, but terms bar AI/ML use of API data since Nov 2024 | Activities only | Contractual: unfit for an AI connector |
| **Business GCDP / Terra** | Business-only; Terra is paid | Push delivery, Training API (workout push), Courses | Not available to individuals |

The Personal API key is created on Garmin Connect web. Its expiry can be set to 30 days, 90 days, 180 days or 1 year, and it is shown only once. It is exchanged for an OAuth2 token that lasts 24 hours and comes with no refresh token. Coverage is Activity (JSON and .FIT), Health and Women's Health, and delivery is pull-only. **Training (pushing workouts to the watch) and Courses remain business-only** ([Garmin personal FAQ snippet](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/personal/personal-faq/); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/22/garmin-personal-api/)). One implementer documents the token exchange: POST the key to `/personal-api-key/token`, and re-exchange it on any 401. They map 26 endpoints, including an unpublished VO2max path, and found **undocumented history caps**: 28 days for steps and stress, 31 for hydration, and 365 for HRV and weight. Longer ranges return a bare 400 ([Samiu1/garmin-connect-mcp](https://github.com/Samiu1/garmin-connect-mcp)). Official rate limits, and whether Training Readiness or Training Status are exposed at all, remain **[unverified]**.

The unofficial route is richer but fragile. Around **17 March 2026**, Garmin changed its auth flow and added Cloudflare TLS fingerprinting, which broke third-party logins ([python-garminconnect #332](https://github.com/cyberjunky/python-garminconnect/issues/332); [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-web/433892/tls-fingerprinting-blocks-third-party-clients/2049649)). garth is now deprecated ([garth docs](https://garth.readthedocs.io/)). python-garminconnect 0.3.x recovered by mimicking the iOS mobile SSO through curl_cffi, with refreshable tokens and MFA support ([README](https://github.com/cyberjunky/python-garminconnect)). The most popular MCP, Taxuspt/garmin_mcp, wraps it with 110+ tools ([GitHub](https://github.com/Taxuspt/garmin_mcp)), so it inherits that fragility. Strava's November 2024 agreement **prohibits using API data for AI/ML**, and coverage reports further tightening in 2026 ([DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/11/stravas-changes-to-kill-off-apps.html); [Strava community](https://communityhub.strava.com/developers-api-7/ai-inference-with-strava-data-is-it-prohibited-under-the-new-api-agreement-13256)). That rules Strava out as a source.

**Is MCP the only or the best way?** It is the best *delivery protocol* to Claude and similar assistants, because it is a standard, it supports tool calls, and it works with Claude Desktop and Claude Code. It is not the only option. A scheduled export of daily markdown or CSV summaries into a project, or intervals.icu's own MCP servers ([intervals-icu-mcp](https://pypi.org/project/intervals-icu-mcp)), are workable zero-code fallbacks. The architectural point matters more. **Put sync and storage behind the MCP, not inside it.** When the MCP reads a local database, login frequency drops (the main ban vector), the 28-day caps stop mattering because history accumulates locally, responses are fast, and a Garmin outage leaves the assistant usable.

## The FR955 emits a deep but bounded data set

The FR955 (June 2022) carries the 2022-generation Garmin/Firstbeat suite with an Elevate Gen4 sensor. It **has no skin-temperature sensor and no ECG**, which first arrived on the FR970 in 2025 ([Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/blog/fitness/the-difference-between-garmin-forerunner-965-and-970/); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/05/15/garmin-forerunner-970-comparison-965)). Any skin-temperature field will therefore be empty, and so will features that depend on the HRM 600, such as running economy and step speed loss **[unverified]**.

Two practical consequences follow. First, store the **components** of Garmin composites (sleep score, HRV, acute load, recovery time), not just the score. The assistant can then explain why readiness is low. Second, most Training-Status-family metrics are probably reachable only through the unofficial library. Treat them as enrichment that can disappear without notice.

## Open formulas cover almost everything worth computing

Every load metric has the same shape: duration multiplied by a threshold-anchored intensity weight. **NP** is the fourth root of the mean of the fourth powers of 30-second rolling power. **IF = NP/FTP**, and **TSS = t·NP·IF/(FTP·3600)·100** ([TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/normalized-power-intensity-factor-training-stress/)). **rTSS = hours × IF² × 100**, using grade-adjusted pace against threshold pace. The NGP grade model is proprietary ([TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/running-training-stress-score-rtss-explained/)). Minetti's polynomial gives an open alternative for grade cost ([Minetti 2002](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.01177.2001)). **Banister TRIMP** = Σ Δt·HRr·0.64·e^(k·HRr), where k = 1.92 for men and 1.67 for women ([GoldenCheetah](https://bazaar.launchpad.net/~ubuntu-branches/ubuntu/saucy/goldencheetah/saucy/view/head:/src/TRIMPPoints.cpp); [Morton 1990](https://doi.org/10.1152/jappl.1990.69.3.1171)). Session RPE load is RPE × minutes ([Foster 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11708692/)). Because a runner with wrist power has HR, pace and power on every run, the connector can compute HR-TRIMP for every session and power-based RSS when Critical Power is known. Garmin's EPOC "Training Load" should be kept as a separate series and never mixed into TSS.

The **PMC** follows directly. CTL_t = CTL_{t−1} + (load − CTL_{t−1})/42, and ATL uses 7 days in place of 42. TSB is yesterday's CTL minus yesterday's ATL. Ramp rate is the 7-day change in CTL ([TrainingPeaks PMC](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/the-science-of-the-performance-manager/)). Rest days must count as zero, and the first ~6 weeks should be marked as unreliable. **ACWR is contested.** Coupled windows inflate the ratio, and random chronic loads predict injury just as well ([Lolli 2019](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/15/921.full); [Impellizzeri 2021](https://iris.univr.it/retrieve/e34cfb98-e922-4c2f-aab5-18583ab7e31b/Impellizzeri_What%20Role%20Do%20Chronic%20Workloads%20Play_SportMed_2021.pdf)). If shown at all, it should be the uncoupled EWMA version, labelled "load change" and never "injury risk". TSB "productive zones" and ramp-rate limits are practitioner heuristics with no peer-reviewed validation.

For **HRV**, use ln(RMSSD). Compare its 7-day rolling mean against a baseline of roughly 60 days that excludes the most recent week, and flag when the gap exceeds a configurable 0.5 SD. Add the 7-day CV as a second marker of maladaptation ([Plews 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0071-8); [AIS guide](https://www.ais.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0013/1130260/HRV-BPG_FINAL.pdf); [Plews 2012](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs00421-012-2354-4.pdf)). The exact SWC multiplier is not standardised **[unverified]**. No composite **readiness** score has peer-reviewed validation. The defensible build is a z-score of each input against the athlete's own baseline (ln RMSSD, inverted RHR, sleep, inverted ATL), combined with transparent weights and shown with its components. The AIS guide supports pairing HRV with subjective wellness, and subjective measures track load more consistently than objective ones ([Saw 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-094758)).

For **performance**, the options are well documented. Daniels-Gilbert VDOT and Riegel (T2 = T1·(D2/D1)^1.06, with exponents of 1.03-1.15 depending on the athlete) are closed-form ([Riegel 1981](https://www.jstor.org/stable/27850427); [Vickers 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y)). **Critical speed** with D′, fitted from 2-15 minute maximal efforts, is the most physiologically grounded. Skiba's W′bal model tracks the D′ reserve during a session ([Jones 2010](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181d9cf7f); [Skiba 2012](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3182517a80)). A Runalyze-style Marathon Shape adjustment corrects predictions that ignore training volume. Rounding out the set: Seiler's three-zone distribution plus the Treff polarization index ([Treff 2019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2019.00707)), EF and Pa:HR decoupling (under 5% is good on steady runs of 60 minutes or more) ([TrainingPeaks/Friel](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/aerobic-endurance-and-decoupling/)), and Foster monotony and strain ([Foster 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9662690/)). The only metrics that cannot be replicated are the proprietary Firstbeat ones: Body Battery, Training Readiness weights, and EPOC-based VO2max. For those, ingest Garmin's values as-is.

## Recommended architecture and phased build order

The architecture has five components. (1) **Ingest**: a Python sync daemon run by cron every 2-6 hours. Its primary adapter is the Personal API, which exchanges the key for a token, caches it, and re-exchanges on 401. It also has an optional python-garminconnect adapter, isolated so that its failures only log warnings, and a one-time GDPR-export importer. (2) **Store**: SQLite or DuckDB holding raw JSON, FIT files on disk, FIT data parsed with the official `garmin-fit-sdk` ([fit-python-sdk](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/garmin/fit-python-sdk/main/README.md)) into session, lap, record and RR tables, and a dated thresholds table so historical load stays correct when FTP or LTHR change. (3) **Analytics**: pure functions run after each sync that write daily derived tables. (4) **MCP server**: Python using the MCP SDK, over stdio for Claude Desktop/Code, with 12-20 intent-level tools that only read the database. (5) **Memory**: a small athlete-context store for goals, preferences, injuries and coaching decisions, copying AthleteData's best idea at almost no cost.

SQLite schema + idempotent upserts · 5. FIT parser (session/lap/record/hrv) · 6. Cron sync + `refresh_data` tool | Gets official data flowing; local history starts accumulating past the 28-day caps |

Several items are **unverified or risky** and should be validated early, as spikes in Phase 0. The first is the Personal API's real endpoint list and rate limits, and whether VO2max and Training-Status-family data are present. The second is the FR955 FIT message set, specifically which running-dynamics fields are present without a pod. The third is the GDPR export's contents. The last is whether python-garminconnect still logs in at the time of building. Strava should not be used as a data source at all.

## Conclusion

The October 2026 Personal API changes the build-versus-buy answer. Until then, every free Garmin MCP relied on reverse-engineered logins that Garmin showed in March 2026 it can break overnight. Now the core feed can be official and free, and the unofficial library is demoted to an enrichment that is allowed to fail. Paid connectors earn their fees from derived analytics, memory and convenience, not from data access. Almost all of those analytics rest on published, decades-old sports-science formulas, so a personal build can be more transparent than the commercial ones by showing every component behind each score.

The two real limits are structural. Garmin keeps watch write-back (the Training API) for business partners, and Firstbeat's composites stay black boxes. The pragmatic stance is to ingest Garmin's proprietary scores as reference values next to open, explainable equivalents, and to let the assistant reason over both. That pairing, a Garmin number plus a transparent rebuild of it, is the feature no paid product currently offers.
